- three r186 lighting divides by pi: ambient and directional intensities need a factor of Math.PI to reach the palette colour on top faces.
- Vertex colours skip sRGB conversion; pass them through `new THREE.Color(hex)` so they land in linear space like material colours.
- Reference measures true isometric (pitch 35.264): a top face is 44 px wide and 25.4 px tall, and one block step is 29 px (rim 4 + side 25), so tileH is about 1.14 against the cube's 0.577 side ratio. 2d sprites use 128 px tiles (x2.909 of the reference): top face 128x74, block step 84.
- Gemini draws the away-facing ramp (d=w/n) as the toward-facing one on the first two tries; the third try holds the orientation but splits the slope into two planes. The 2d ramp-w/ramp-n sprites are weak; draw a placeholder wedge if they read wrong in the island.

- In true isometric, cell (x+1, z+1, h+1) projects onto (x, z, h), so screen position alone never fixes a tile's height; decide h from visible cliff faces and occlusion.
- The reference terraces step 38.4 px (1.5 cube edges), not whole cubes; home.json encodes that as view.tileH 1.5 rather than fractional h.
- The promo artist rescaled the water group about 1.35x and nudged the lone block 9 px off-lattice; exact pixel matching of those pieces is impossible on one grid.
- Stålberg restarts Bad North islands on WFC failure and calls WFC "pretty bad for long, thin things"; bridges and paths belong to a rule pass, not the solver.
- DeBroglie's ConnectedConstraint bans placements that break a path and needs backtracking; on a 200-cell grid, flood fill plus repair after generation is cheaper and exact.
- 2d painter's order: every draw call carries the key (diag, layer, level, tie). diag is x + z of the cell; tiles use layer 0 and level = the sprite's top-plane world y, so a column draws bottom block, top sprite, then its fill band. Decor and the hero use layer 1 and diag of the front-most touched cell (sample points at +-0.3 of the anchor) whose top is not above them, so a hero stepping onto a cell draws after that cell's tile; the tie is x + z of the anchor, the hero sorts last in its bucket. Tiles never overlap within one diagonal, so no finer key is needed.
- 2d scale: sprite px per world unit P = 64 / project(1,0,0).sx = 90.5; home's zoom 31.33 gives scale 0.3462 and the renderer lands the REFERENCE.md checkpoints to 0.1 px (well 32.6,214.3; lone block 342.8,214.3; plaza 232.0,227.1). A 0.33 scale scores better (NCC 0.722) only because the water group is drawn 1.35x in the promo; the grid stays at the measured zoom.
- 2d cliff columns: stacking 84 px block sprites at tileH 1.5 leaves visible block seams and texture mismatches; draw one bottom block, the top sprite, and a band of rows 112..120 of the top sprite stretched over the gap. The sides are vertical stripes, so the stretch is invisible. Columns stop at the lower front neighbour (void neighbours look up to three cells ahead for land), which reproduces the reference's one-slab rims at the well and bridge end.
- An away-facing ramp (d = w or n) in true isometric at tileH >= 1 hides its slope: the slope is steeper than the view ray, so only the two earth faces and the grass lip show. 2d/gen/ramp.mjs builds ramp-w from sheared tile-grass faces and mirrors it to ramp-n at $0.
